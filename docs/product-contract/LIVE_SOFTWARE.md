# Live Software — RENUABL Business

Martin, 8 Nov 2026. Prompted by Today's deal feed.

## Principle
RENUABL is live software, not static software.

- **Static software** keeps records and waits. Every action starts with a person deciding to look. A pipeline is a picture of work someone still has to do.
- **Live software** does the work, and its screens show that work happening. Records still exist, but as a by-product: the log of what was done, not the place where work is done.

Sol does the work. The owner and the team oversee it and step in where their judgement changes the outcome. Every new screen is built this way.

## The three questions
Every screen answers, in this order:
1. **What has been done?** By Sol, by the team, by the customer.
2. **What is about to happen?** What Sol will do next, and when.
3. **What is needed from me?** Only what a person must do, routed to the person whose job it is.

A screen that only answers "here are your records" is not finished.

## Give notice, don't ask permission
The core interaction is a countdown:

> "I'm going to do this, at this time, unless you step in."

- Sol shows his intent: the email, the order, the booking, the stage move.
- It happens when the countdown ends.
- The person can change the time, pause it, edit it or do it now.
- Each business sets the default wait. As it comes to trust Sol, the wait shortens, until it effectively disappears.

Ask permission only where an action can't be undone and the risk is real, for example spending the business's money or something irreversible with a customer. Everything else gets notice.

## Sol shows his work
Live and wrong is worse than static and out of date. So:
- Every action says what Sol based it on: the customer's own words, the record, the sources.
- Everything he did is written on the deal's record, with his name.
- He says plainly when he doesn't know, and asks the team instead of guessing.
- He never promises, discounts or decides on the business's behalf.

## Calm, not restless
Live software can make owners anxious. Keep it calm:
- Most things read as handled. "Needs you" is rare and means it.
- Movement on screen means something changed, never decoration.
- After time away, say what happened while you were gone, in a few lines.

## Time is part of the interface
Countdowns, "since you were last here", ordering by what happened most recently, and when the next thing will happen. Show a static total only where it's the actual answer to a question.

## When Sol works
There are no built-in hours. Each business sets them: always on, or its own days and times. Every action Sol takes on its own happens inside them.

## Records become views
The pipeline board, customer lists and reports stay, as views to check the whole picture. Nobody should have to keep them up to date: Sol moves deals, logs calls and fills in details from what happened.

## Applies everywhere, not just sales
Each area has its "I'm about to…" version. For example:
- **Sales:** "I'll reply to Priya at 8:05 with: …"
- **Procurement:** "I'll order this stock from the wholesaler at 3pm."
- **Installation:** "I'll book Ben's install for Tuesday and let him know."
- **Service:** "Mei reported an inverter fault. I've asked the installer for a visit and told her we're on it."

## What it changes
- **Pricing.** Sol credits are charged per answer, which suits software that waits to be asked. A Sol that works on his own does work nobody asked for. Over time, charge for the work done (per lead handled, per deal won) rather than per question.
- **How success is measured.** Less time from the owner for each deal won, and faster first replies to customers. Not screens visited or records kept.
