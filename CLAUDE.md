# RENUABL — working notes for Claude

## Read first

- `docs/design/` holds the approved visual design (brand sheet, desktop + mobile customer screens, installer portal). **Match it.** It overrides any earlier styling in this repo.

- `docs/product-contract/` is the UX contract (master prompt + four surface specs). When anything is ambiguous, prefer simplicity and those principles over adding features.
- `docs/BUSINESS_MODEL.md`: the household, not the lead or the job, is the core entity. Solar is product 1 of a catalogue.

## Product rules to preserve

- RENUABL recommends one system, sized from the customer's electricity bill. No catalogues.
- The customer uploads their latest bill on "About your home"; Claude reads it server-side (`src/app/api/bill/route.ts`, `src/lib/server/bill-reader.ts`) and `src/lib/domain/bill.ts` validates the figures. Ask only for usage and prices, never personal details, and don't store the file. If Claude is overloaded (429/5xx), `readWithClaude` retries once, then tries `ANTHROPIC_FALLBACK_MODEL` (default `claude-sonnet-5`). On phones the main option is "Take a photo" (opens the camera), with "Upload a file" for PDFs and screenshots; desktop shows "Upload bill". The bill is required: nobody continues without one (Ask Revo must never say otherwise). Continue stays pressable without a bill: pressing it scrolls to the upload with "Please upload your latest bill to continue" and only then shows "Don't have your bill handy right now?" (`BillLater`, `/api/follow-up`), which takes an email: HubSpot contact + "Finish later: no bill yet" note (home, ad source) and a `finishLaterEmail` link back; hidden once a bill is read. The bill must be for the home entered: the address is sent with the bill (`addressForBillCheck`), Claude answers only yes / no / unclear for the supply address (never returns it), and only a clear "no" turns the bill away (`addressMismatchMessage`).
- Sizing (`recommendSystem`), explained to customers under "How we worked this out" on the system step (`sizingExplanation`; keep it in step): Essential = the least solar that covers the home's yearly use at the year's average sunshine, never below the 5 kW minimum system (`ASSUMPTIONS.minSystemKw`); wanting a battery doesn't change it. Rule of thumb (`solarDay`): the home uses `ASSUMPTIONS.baseSelfConsumption` (40%, PLACEHOLDER; 30–40% is typical) of its solar as it's made, never more than its daily use; the rest of its use is after dark; the 60% surplus charges the battery, then exports. With a battery (Recommended, Maximum) it's sized for winter (`winterYield`: NASA's June–August sunshine against the year, else `ASSUMPTIONS.winterSunShare` 0.5): `panelsForBattery` fills the smallest hybrid inverter to `COSTING.maxArrayToHybridWithBattery` (150%, PLACEHOLDER until the CER/network wording is confirmed with Primero; 133% without a battery) whose winter surplus covers the after-dark use; the battery (`batteryForSolar`) is the smallest 8 kWh-module size whose 90% usable holds that winter after-dark use (one size up for backup). Savings (`estimateOutcome`) use the same `solarDay` at the year's average. Customers never choose panel counts or battery sizes. The options are Essential = solar only, Recommended = solar + battery, Maximum = the same solar and battery + a smart EV charger (the EV charger is only in Maximum; customers can still switch it on or off on the system step). Expanding existing solar: a smaller battery / the battery / the battery + EV charger (battery sized to the bill's after-dark purchases, `batteryNeeded`; top-up panels use `batteryReadySolar` headroom).
- The battery question is "Would you like a battery?" (a sale now, not "later"). Yes leads with Recommended, No with Essential.
- Existing solar (the bill shows exports): ask its size, or "I'm not sure", then "Replace or expand?". Expanding (or a known size) keeps the panels and sells a battery in every option, with extra panels only if exports can't fill it, and always shows `EXPAND_DISCLAIMER` (the inverter is unknown until the call), with an optional upload of the inverter's front and its model/serial sticker, which Claude reads (`/api/inverter`) for make, model and kW; photos aren't stored. Replacing sizes a new system to estimated real use (`realAnnualUse`, placeholder self-use share) and says it's an estimate. Logic: `src/lib/domain/existing-solar.ts`.
- One matched installer by default; alternatives only behind a secondary link. Customers never see the word "installer": they're **installation partners** (code, the installer portal and staff notes keep "installer"). Step 4 opens with "Finding your installation partner.", about 5 seconds of matching steps that are all true of the match, played once per address (`matchedPostcode`). Don't claim anything about timing the customer hasn't given yet.
- The customer picks the install day only; installers arrive 7am–9am (`INSTALL_ARRIVAL`), never a customer-chosen time. New systems in Victoria get dates from `SOLAR_VIC_LEAD_DAYS` (21, PLACEHOLDER) out, for the Solar Victoria application and its 7–10 business-day approval (`installLeadDays`, `useInstallLead`), and a chosen date says `SOLAR_VIC_DATE_NOTE`; otherwise `LEAD_TIME_DAYS` (7). The 15-minute confirmation call (confirmation, not sales) comes before the deposit.
- Intelligence is branded "Ask Revo", never labelled "AI". The chat is a conversation (`useRevoChat` in `services/revo-chat.ts`, kept in sessionStorage for the visit, shared by every Ask Revo button, "Start again" clears it): Revo opens with `REVO_GREETINGS` for the screen, remembers the last 10 exchanges, may ask one short question back, never asks for name, email, phone or address, and points pre-flow visitors to "start with your address". Revo also sits bottom right on the home page and guides (`page` companion, `REVO_PAGE_LINES`, bubble tucks away after 7 s). Revo (the mascot) is a persistent guide on every flow step (`RevoGuide` in `flow-shell.tsx`, the `revo` variant of `AskRenuabl`): a speech bubble with one line from `revoLine` (`src/lib/domain/revo.ts`, tested) that narrates the step and backs up each choice, plus the avatar; tapping opens Ask Revo for that screen (`revoContext`). Bottom right on desktop; above the sticky Continue on phones, where the bubble tucks away after 7 s (each new line brings it back). Lines stay short, never invent figures or claims. Answers come from Claude via `/api/ask` (`ASK_MODEL`, default `claude-sonnet-5`, using `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY`), grounded only in `ASK_FACTS` and the customer's own answers (`src/lib/domain/ask-knowledge.ts`); keep those facts in step with these rules. Without a key, or if Claude fails, the reviewed canned answers in `src/lib/services/ask.ts` are used.
- No traditional website navigation (no Home / Energy / Savings / … link bar). Customer screens show only the wordmark and account; people move through guided steps, the My RENUABL home cards and the mobile bottom tabs.
- Reserving is free: the reserve step collects name, mobile and email (no payment) and sends the lead to HubSpot (`/api/reserve`, `HUBSPOT_PRIVATE_APP_TOKEN`). Every lead (reservation or "no bill yet") is also emailed to staff (`alertNewLead`, `newLeadEmail`; `LEAD_ALERT_EMAILS`, comma-separated, default martin@renuabl.com.au); it never blocks the lead. The customer is emailed the order breakdown and next steps (`src/lib/domain/emails.ts`, sent via Twilio SendGrid when `SENDGRID_API_KEY` is set, else Resend (`RESEND_API_KEY`), in `src/lib/server/email.ts`, with `EMAIL_FROM`) with an install-day invite; a call booked in the in-app calendar is noted in HubSpot and emailed with its own invite (`/api/call`). The confirmation page offers add-to-calendar (.ics + Google, `src/lib/domain/calendar.ts`) and only says an email was sent when it was. Emails never block a reservation. The $499 refundable deposit (`ASSUMPTIONS.deposit`) is taken after the confirmation call via Stripe's embedded checkout form on `/deposit` (`ui_mode: "form"`, API version `STRIPE_API_VERSION`, Stripe.js loaded from js.stripe.com/dahlia in `src/lib/services/stripe-form.ts`, `NEXT_PUBLIC_STRIPE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`); setup steps in `STRIPE_INTEGRATION_TODO.md`. The partner calendar stays in-app (no ServiceM8).
- Pricing is costed, not estimated: `src/lib/domain/catalogue.ts` holds the chosen products at supplier cost (AWM Clayton price list: Jinko 475 W panel, Sungrow string inverters for solar-only, Sigenergy SigenStor controller + 8 kWh modules for batteries, Antai rail and tin/tile kits). `src/lib/domain/costing.ts` builds the bill of materials by rule: inverter = smallest with array ≤ 133% of its rating (150% for a hybrid with a battery, `arrayRatio`); rail = (panel width + 0.1 m) × 2 per portrait panel, plus a 10% buffer on flat roofs (to bridge arrays) or 5% otherwise, in 4.8 m lengths; arrays are allowed for as 2, or 3 above 18 panels (+$150 third-array installation); one tin/tile kit per 2 kW (roof "not sure" → tile); flat roofs always get the tin kit plus Clenergy universal Kliplok interfaces (CLNER-I-34, 2 per panel + 2 per array), since most are Kliplok, and ask "How would you like your panels?": laid flat (default; sized with no tilt gain so a little larger) or tilted (adds an Antai 10–15° tilt kit per 2 kW plus $15/panel installation; tilting depends on roof space and looks, both checked on the call, with one short note, not paragraphs); BOS = DC label kit, battery label kit (when there's a battery), 10 MC4 pairs, 4 panel clips per panel in packs of 100, and an AC isolator sized to the inverter (`acIsolatorFor`): NHP 40 A 2-pole single phase / 3-pole three phase while the inverter's output is ≤ 32 A a phase, a 63 A for bigger single-phase inverters (8 kW and up; the 63 A is a stand-in 3-pole until a 63 A 2-pole is on the price list); solar install 30c/W (+$400 double storey); battery install $1,800 per stack with modules beyond six at $1,800 ÷ 6 each; EV charger install $1,000; +$150 only when a three-phase inverter is fitted. Install costs are ex GST. Then +20% margin, +GST. "About your home" asks roof, storeys and phase (phase "not sure" → single). Add-ons without a confirmed price (`price: null` in `ADD_ONS`: until costed, smart switchboard (Smart Home Integration was removed: too vague to sell); "Blackout Backup" (`home-backup`, `needsBattery`) is a priced extra: wiring backup circuits to the battery, `COSTING.backupCircuitsInstall` $1,000 ex GST installation (the gateway is already in every battery), offered and priced only with a battery; the battery sheet explains it (`backupNote`) and the circuits are agreed on the call. Every price carries `PRICE_INCLUDES` ("Includes installation, the inverter, electrical work, commissioning and GST"); the system step shows the panel wattage ("7.1 kW · 475 W panels"), the battery maker, and "Panels we use" / "Batteries we use" lists (maker · size · warranty) from `PANEL_RANGE`/`BATTERY_RANGE` in `catalogue.ts` (add up to three panels as they're priced; warranties marked VERIFY until checked on the datasheets); the exact equipment for the home is chosen in the design and shown on the call) are "discuss on my call": no price, not in the total, listed in the basket, HubSpot note and email. Home electrification (reverse-cycle heating & cooling, heat pump hot water) is `comingSoon`: a "Coming soon" badge on the Extras step, "Let me know when it's available" (`price.interested`: not priced, not discussed on the call, listed as "Coming soon" in the basket and email and "Interested (coming soon)" in HubSpot). Only give an add-on a price once its supply and install are confirmed. Heat pumps will need their own rebates (federal STCs, Victorian VEECs, Solar Victoria hot water rebate) before they're sold.
- Supplier price imports (`/admin` "Supplier prices"; `price-import.ts`, tested against the real AWM list in `__fixtures__/awm-price-list.xlsx`): staff upload an Excel (`server/xlsx.ts`, no library) or CSV price list with the supplier's name; rows are matched to catalogue SKUs (`catalogueEntries`, prices ex GST, AWM's "26.0c/w ($123.50)" read as $123.50), the changes shown (≥ 30% in amber) and nothing changes until "Apply". Products not in a file keep their price.
- Rebates (`src/lib/domain/rebates.ts`) are separate customer-facing lines in green, named "STC Solar Incentive", "BSTC Battery Incentive" and "Solar Victoria Rebate" (+ "Solar Victoria Battery Rebate"), shown on the system step's price card (price before rebates, each rebate, price after; VIC new systems without the toggle on see "you may also be eligible") and at checkout: federal solar STCs, federal battery STCs, and Solar Victoria (VIC addresses only, behind a customer toggle, with an eligibility link). The Solar Victoria interest-free loan is a second, always-visible toggle (switching it on switches on the rebate it depends on) that lowers the upfront cost, not the price. For expansions of existing solar the card explains that Solar Victoria doesn't apply rather than disappearing. Rules are loaded daily from the CER and Solar Victoria (`/api/rebates`, `src/lib/server/rebate-rates.ts`, parsers tested against saved page text in `src/lib/domain/__fixtures__`), falling back to `VERIFIED_RATES` (hand-checked 27 Sep 2026) per item. STCs use the CER postcode zone table (`zone-ratings.ts`), deeming years for the install year, and the battery factor for the install date, tapered 100% ≤14 kWh / 60% ≤28 / 15% ≤50 kWh. The STC $ price isn't published by the CER: set `STC_PRICE`. Sunshine comes from NASA POWER for the home's coordinates (`/api/sunshine`).

## Architecture rules

- Business logic lives in `src/lib/domain` (pure TS, unit tested). Don't put it in components.
- UI reads and writes only through `src/lib/services/*`; mock data stays in `src/lib/mock`.
- Use the semantic color tokens (`bg-canvas`, `bg-surface`, `text-ink`, `text-muted`, `border-line`, `text-positive`, …) rather than raw colors so both themes work.
- `cn` only joins classes (no tailwind-merge): a `className` can't override a component's own display or colour classes, so wrap it (e.g. a `hidden lg:block` div) instead. Disabled buttons use solid muted colours, never transparency.
- Small text links and controls get a ~44px touch area with the `tap-area` utility (in `globals.css`). The mobile menu (`MobileMenu`) offers "Start with your address" and "My RENUABL", plus a quieter "Become a partner" (for installers and retailers). The home page header also has "Become a partner" (desktop) / "Partners" (mobile), home page only, never mid-purchase. In preview, partner sign-up offers a sample certificate of currency (`public/samples/`) for testing; the live server rejects it.
- Mobile-first, but build deliberate desktop layouts (see `FlowStep`, `InstallerShell`) rather than stretching mobile.
- Don't put `position: fixed` elements inside an animated (`transform`) ancestor, because it breaks fixed positioning. `FlowStep` renders its sticky CTA outside the animated section for this reason.
- Server pages that depend on "today" call `await connection()` so they render per request.
